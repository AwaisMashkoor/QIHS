Unit 1The key shift
The Level 8 distinction

A person can receive competent care from several separate services — and still experience poor care overall.

Even if every service does its own part well, the overall experience can fail at the joins.

Good parts do not automatically add up to good care.

Let this sit. The move is away from "someone did a bad job" towards a system problem: individually reasonable actions can still produce an incoherent whole. Do not claim anything about how well Richard's services perform — the point holds without it. This is the bridge into what integrated care means.

Unit 1Evaluation
Why am I telling you this?

When a programme says "admissions fell 10%", ask three things

Compared with what?

Unmet need. Better coordination may uncover needs that were missed. Better care can therefore mean more recorded service use at first.

Over how long?

Time. Organisational change takes years to mature. A short evaluation may ask whether a programme worked before it had time to work.

What else could explain it?

Who was chosen. If people are selected because their service use has recently been unusually high, some of that use may fall anyway. Be careful about claiming cause.

So fewer hospital admissions cannot be our only definition of good integrated care. You will use these three questions again this afternoon.

Open with why: a Level 8 student should not accept "admissions fell 10%" without asking these. They are not learning evaluation methodology for its own sake; they are learning not to make naive claims. Plain language throughout — do not say "regression to the mean" unless asked. The afternoon deck puts three claims about the House of Care in front of them and asks exactly these questions; tell them it is coming.

Glossary — the words in Richard's story

Plain-English, one line each. Nobody is expected to know these already.

The organisations

  • NHS Scotland — the health service. Free at the point of use. Organised into area health boards that run hospitals, community health and mental health services.
  • Local authority / council — elected local government. Responsible for social work, social care, housing, benefits advice, schools.
  • Social care — help with daily living: washing, dressing, meals, support at home, care homes. Assessed by the council and can be charged for.
  • Third sector — charities, voluntary and community organisations. Advice, advocacy, peer support, activities.
  • Integration authority / Integration Joint Board (IJB) — the body set up in each area, under the 2014 Act, to plan health and social care together.
  • Health and Social Care Partnership (HSCP) — the local arrangement through which integrated services are delivered.

The people

  • GP — general practitioner, the family doctor. First point of contact; refers to specialists.
  • Ward team — the doctors, nurses and others looking after someone while they are in hospital.
  • Community mental health team — NHS team supporting people with mental illness at home rather than in hospital.
  • Social worker — council professional who assesses needs, arranges support and protects people at risk.
  • Housing officer — council or housing association staff who manage tenancies, rent arrears and repairs.
  • Unpaid carer — a family member or friend who provides care without being paid. Has rights to support of their own.
  • Link worker — connects people to non-medical support in the community (social prescribing).

Richard's situation

  • Long-term condition — a health condition that can be managed but not cured, lived with for years, shaping daily life.
  • Schizophrenia — a serious mental illness affecting thinking, perception and mood. Treatable; often long-term, with periods of being well and periods of relapse.
  • Type 2 diabetes — a long-term condition where the body cannot control blood sugar well; managed by diet, activity and often medication.
  • Inpatient unit / admission / discharge — a hospital ward where someone stays for treatment; being taken in; being sent home with a plan.
  • Tenancy — the legal agreement to rent a home. Rent arrears — unpaid rent that is owed. Both can put the home at risk.
  • Wellbeing hub / community wellbeing service — a local service bringing together help with housing, money, health and community activities in one place.

The ideas

  • Integrated care — health and social care planned and delivered so they work as one around the person.
  • Fragmentation — care that falls apart at the joins between services.
  • Continuity — whether information, the plan and relationships hold together over time.
  • Care plan — a written record of someone's needs, goals and the support agreed.
  • Multidisciplinary team (MDT) — professionals from different disciplines working together on one person's care.
  • Person-centred care — care organised around what matters to the person, with them as a partner. Unit 4.
  • Anticipatory care planning — planning ahead for what might happen, before it becomes a crisis. Unit 8.
  • House of Care — the framework for doing all of this. This afternoon.
