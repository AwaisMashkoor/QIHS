Next Steps / Assessment 1The task
The assessment brief

The task in plain words

The audience

Patients and families

People offered a place at the long-term condition management clinic at their local health centre.

The leaflet also needs to make sense to someone picking it up in the waiting room.

The content

Three requirements

  • Define integrated care and what it can achieve for people with long-term conditions.
  • Define person-centred care and what it means for someone with a long-term condition.
  • Present the House of Care and explain how each part supports collaborative, person-centred care.
The submission

One page and references

  • One A4 leaflet page. The handbook does not specify a leaflet word count.
  • A separate list of 3 or 4 up-to-date, peer-reviewed sources from course texts or equivalent sources.
  • In-text citations are not required on the leaflet.
  • Your own wording. The handbook prohibits AI-generated assessment content. Complete the required declaration.
Would the reader understand what the clinic offers and how it could support them?

The audience determines how the information is presented. Explain the concepts accurately using language patients and families can understand. Include both a definition and its relevance to living with a long-term condition.

The handbook specifies one A4 leaflet page, with no leaflet word count. Do not treat the example as a minimum, maximum or recommended length. The content needs enough explanation to meet the brief, with a readable layout.

The separate reference page sits outside the one-page leaflet. The handbook does not require in-text citations on the leaflet. It does require a short source list and prohibits AI from generating assessment content.

Source: UWS module handbook, Assessment Component 1.

Next Steps / Assessment 1The content
Applying the content criteria

What the leaflet needs to explain

Integrated care / 15 marks available

Services working together

Explain how health, social care and community services coordinate care and support around the person.

Then explain the possible benefit: less repetition, better coordinated support and help before problems become more serious.

Person-centred care / 15 marks available

What matters to the person

Explain how care responds to someone’s needs, preferences and everyday life.

Show what this means in practice: being listened to, understanding options and making decisions together.

House of Care / 30 marks available

Each part has a purpose

Include the roof, both walls, the foundation and the care and support planning conversation at the centre.

Explain how each part helps the clinic support the reader. Connect the diagram to practical examples.

Readability: use everyday words, explain unfamiliar terms and keep sentences manageable. Addressing the reader as “you” is one useful approach.

Each concept needs an explanation and a clear link to the reader’s experience. The House of Care includes four supporting parts around the central conversation, so this deck refers to five visible parts.

The Scottish model places organisational systems in the roof, informed and involved people in one wall, professional partnership in the other and support beyond medicine in the foundation. The foundation also involves organising and sustaining that support through appropriate resources.

Plain language is a communication choice, not a fixed list of banned words or a compulsory sentence length.

Sources: UWS module handbook; ALLIANCE, The House of Care Model; The Health Foundation (2014).

Next Steps / Assessment 1Common issues
Points to check

Common issues and useful improvements

Dense academic wording

Long paragraphs and unexplained terms can be difficult to follow. Improve it: use everyday words, clear headings and shorter sentences where helpful.

A diagram without explanation

Labels alone do not show how the framework helps. Improve it: connect each part to what it means for the patient or family.

Benefits presented as guarantees

“You will never repeat your story” promises too much. Improve it: explain what coordinated care can help achieve and support the claim.

An unclear introduction

The reader may not know what the leaflet covers. Improve it: state the purpose near the beginning. Choose a layout that makes it easy to find.

Hard-to-read design

Small text, weak contrast and blurred images obscure the message. Improve it: check the finished page at normal A4 reading size.

Contact details without the content

An address and phone number do not explain the required concepts. Improve it: check that the three content requirements on slide 2 are covered.

These are illustrative issues to check against the rubric. They are not accounts of particular students or claims about previous marking results.

The assessment requires accurate content as well as suitable presentation. Practical design suggestions help communicate that content; the handbook’s marking criteria remain the basis of assessment.

Glossary

  • Long-term condition: a condition that lasts over time and may require ongoing care and support.
  • Integrated care: health, social care and community services coordinating their work around the person.
  • Person-centred care: care shaped around the person’s needs and preferences, with decisions made together.
  • House of Care: a framework showing what supports the care and support planning conversation: organisational systems, informed and involved people, professionals working in partnership, and support beyond medicine.
  • Care and support planning: preparing for a conversation, discussing what matters, agreeing a plan together and reviewing it.
  • Peer-reviewed: assessed by relevant specialists before publication. A recognised publisher or organisation does not by itself establish peer review.
  • Cite Them Right Harvard: the author–date referencing style used at UWS. The details needed depend on the type of source. See the UWS referencing guide.
