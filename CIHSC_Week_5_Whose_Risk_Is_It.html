Whose risk is it?

“I want to go home. I have been in that house since 1975 and I know where everything is. I want to manage my own stairs and get out to the lunch club on a Tuesday, and I want to see Jean. I know I might fall again. I fell last time and I am still here. What I do not want is Anne organising her whole life round me, because she has her own life and I have seen what that does to people.” — Margaret, 84
Margaret has capacity for this decision. She is not delirious, she understands the options and the foreseeable risks, and she has said what she wants. Nothing about being 84, having fallen, or being identified as living with frailty changes who makes this decision.

Your task: these are ten things actually said in and around a discharge meeting. Sort each one. Start by asking whether it is a risk at all, because several of them are not: some are gaps in the service, some are assumptions about somebody else’s work, and one is a score being used as a decision. Then press Compare to see how we would sort it, who has authority, and what would have to be true. On a touchscreen, tap a card then tap a column.
